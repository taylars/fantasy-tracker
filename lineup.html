Reading your roster…

Proj is the points a player is expected to score this week, under your league's scoring. The small numbers under it, and the bar, are his likely range: a bad week to a good one.

Actual fills in once his game kicks off — what he has scored so far, straight from your league — and the dot on his bar marks it: green above his range, red below it, dark inside it. Sleeper's projection has no range, so there it is green if he beat Sleeper's number and red if he fell short. From kickoff he is locked, as he is in Sleeper, and the page will not suggest moving him.

Past weeks are in the week picker. Each shows the lineup you had, what it was projected to score — using only the weeks before it — and what it actually scored, plus what the projected best lineup would have scored instead.

Projection picks whose numbers the page runs on. Simulated is built here from this season's play — snaps, targets, red-zone looks — and the week's betting lines, then played out four thousand times to get the range; click a player to see how his number is put together. Sleeper's is Sleeper's own weekly projection, a single number with no range. Either way, the best lineup and the changes come from the one you pick.

Too close to matter lists starters less than 1.5 points ahead of the best player on your bench who could take the spot. Either is a fine start. On the Simulated projection each change and close call also says how often one player outscores the other — near 50% is a coin flip.

Injuries are live from Sleeper. OUT, IR, PUP and SUSP project at zero; D (doubtful) usually does not play, which pulls his range down; Q is not discounted, because most questionable players play. When someone is out, his share of the targets and carries goes to his team-mates.

Weeks are the ones with betting lines: the coming week once it has them, and on a Monday the week still being played.
